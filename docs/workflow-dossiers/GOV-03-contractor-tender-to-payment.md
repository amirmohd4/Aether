# Workflow Dossier GOV-03 — Government Works Contract: Tender to Final Payment

**Research cut-off:** 17 October 2026  
**Status:** Desk-research dossier; not a jurisdiction-specific legal procedure.  
**Scope:** Government works procurement in India; each department, state, funding source and contract can vary.  
**Why this workflow:** It connects a contractor's commercial pain (bid readiness, execution evidence, bill processing and cash flow) with a government's procurement, engineering and financial controls.

## 1. Outcome

A qualified contractor submits a compliant bid, executes the awarded contract, records work accurately, obtains authorised measurements/certifications, submits complete bills, resolves documented queries and receives payment according to the contract and applicable rules.

Aether must never guarantee tender award or payment, generate fake measurements, bypass evaluation rules or certify physical work it has not inspected.

## 2. Official sources and update discipline

The Department of Expenditure publishes the **Manual for Procurement of Works, Second Edition 2025** and an amendment dated 18 June 2026. Any workflow pack must identify the current applicable manual, amendment, state/department rules, contract conditions and delegated powers.

- Manuals: https://doe.gov.in/en/manuals
- 18 June 2026 amendment: https://doe.gov.in/en/circulars/amendment-manual-procurement-works-second-edition-2025
- Government eProcurement System: https://www.eprocure.gov.in/eprocure/app

These sources establish that procurement is governed by formal procedures and current amendments. They do not establish that every department uses the same portal or that every bill is delayed.

## 3. Workflow map: contractor and department

### Phase A — Discover and qualify a tender

1. Contractor finds the tender, scope, schedule, corrigenda, eligibility criteria, technical requirements, EMD/bid security and submission deadline.
2. Contractor checks registration, experience, turnover, equipment/personnel, declarations and other required qualifications.
3. Contractor decides whether to bid and prepares the technical and financial bid.

**Today’s tools to inventory:** central/state e-procurement portal, tender alerts/aggregators, PDF documents, spreadsheet trackers, shared drives, CA/engineering/tender consultants, digital-signature tools and internal accounting/ERP.

**Aether opportunity:** parse the tender pack into a source-linked requirement checklist; version every corrigendum; compare the contractor's authorised evidence against each requirement; highlight missing/expired/inconsistent evidence; draft the submission index and deadline plan. Human review remains mandatory for pricing, declarations, bid strategy and final submission.

### Phase B — Bid submission and evaluation

4. Contractor uploads required bid files and submits before the deadline.
5. The procuring entity receives and opens bids under the prescribed process.
6. The authorised evaluation team checks eligibility and technical compliance, evaluates financial bids under the tender's rules, documents the evaluation and obtains required approvals.
7. The authority issues the award/acceptance or follows the prescribed next step.

**Aether opportunity for contractor:** preserve the submission receipt and final pack; track official clarifications and response deadlines; compare the received outcome to the tender timeline.

**Aether opportunity for procuring entity:** check document completeness and compile source-linked evaluation material for authorised staff, if formally procured and permitted.

**Boundary:** Aether must not manipulate bids, expose competitors' confidential bids, score beyond the published criteria, influence procurement decisions, or communicate outside authorised channels.

### Phase C — Agreement, security and commencement

8. Contractor completes the required agreement, performance security, insurance, tax/labour documents, declarations and other conditions specified in the contract.
9. Department issues the relevant work order/commencement instruction and confirms required prerequisites.

**Aether opportunity:** create a contract obligation graph with owner, due date, evidence, expiry, dependency and authoritative acknowledgement. Do not assume every listed condition applies to every contract.

### Phase D — Execution, site records and inspections

10. Contractor mobilises labour, equipment and materials; manages drawings, site access, quality records and the work programme.
11. Departmental engineers/authorised staff carry out prescribed supervision, measurements, quality checks and inspections.
12. Changes, deviations, extensions, extra items, hindrances or claims are documented and approved through the applicable contract procedure.

**Today’s tools to inventory:** project-management/ERP software, measurement books or approved digital measurement systems, site diaries, drawings, quality-test records, inspection notes, emails and signed forms.

**Aether opportunity:** tie each milestone to required evidence, due dates, site records, inspection requests and authorised outcomes; flag missing records before bill preparation.

**Boundary:** Aether can organise evidence and identify inconsistencies, but it cannot replace a physical inspection, engineer's professional judgment or a required signed measurement/certificate.

### Phase E — Running bills and payment

13. Contractor compiles a running account/interim bill or other payment claim according to contract terms.
14. Authorised staff verify measured work, contract rates, approved variations, previous payments, taxes, retention, recoveries, advances and other applicable deductions.
15. The competent authorities perform the prescribed checks and sanctions; the bill is passed into the relevant finance/treasury/payment process.
16. Contractor and department reconcile the payment acknowledgement, deductions and outstanding balance.

**Aether opportunity:** a bill-readiness engine that links each claimed line to the contract, approved rate, measurement evidence, prior bill, variation approval and required certificate; deterministic arithmetic checks; duplicate-claim detection; source-linked discrepancy list; authorised query routing; payment-state reconciliation.

**Boundary:** Aether must not invent measurements, alter a certified quantity, apply an unapproved variation, initiate payment without authority or claim payment completion without an authoritative transaction result.

### Phase F — Completion and final settlement

17. Complete required final measurements, completion/quality documentation, handover and defect-related obligations.
18. Submit the final bill and resolve documented queries, recoveries and contractual claims under applicable terms.
19. Obtain required completion/acceptance documents and reconcile the final payment and outstanding obligations.

**Aether opportunity:** track closure prerequisites, expiry/defect-liability dates, final certificates, retention/security release where applicable, and audit-ready contract history.

## 4. The real pain hypotheses to test

Do not presume the pain is “government employees are slow.” Investigate separately:

- Tender requirements are lengthy, scattered or changed by corrigenda.
- Contractor evidence is missing, expired or inconsistent at submission.
- Small contractors lack dedicated tender/billing staff and rely on consultants.
- Site records and contract variations are not linked cleanly to each bill line.
- The bill is held because a required certificate, measurement, sanction, fund allocation or data correction is missing.
- Contractor and department systems show different payment states.
- Manual status follow-up consumes time but does not change the queue.
- Delay is due to budget/fund availability, dispute, quality issue, staffing, procurement rules or contractor-side non-compliance rather than a software gap.

Aether should classify the actual blocker and responsible owner, not promise to remove every delay.

## 5. Who uses and who pays?

| Customer | Job to be done | Potential payer | Product entry point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small/medium contractor | Submit fewer defective bids; track requirements and bills without building a large admin team | Contractor business | Tender pack preflight and deadline/evidence tracker |
| Large contractor / EPC | Coordinate many projects, contracts, milestones, claims and finance hand-offs | COO/CFO/project controls | Multi-project contract graph and bill reconciliation |
| Engineering/project-management consultant | Manage evidence and deadlines across client projects | Consultant or client | Multi-client workflow and audit trail |
| Works department / public works organisation | Improve completeness, traceability and status of contractor cases | Department through formal procurement | Department-side workflow integration, after security and procurement approval |
| System integrator | Deliver a government or enterprise workflow project | SI or agency customer | Aether as orchestration platform/API |

Potential buyers do not equal validated demand. We need to learn whether the existing e-procurement/works/finance systems and consultants already solve the problem adequately.

## 6. Existing software and manual processes to inventory

- Central or state e-procurement portal, including bid receipts and corrigenda.
- Government procurement manuals, tender conditions and contract records.
- Departmental works/project systems and approved measurement-book processes.
- Finance/treasury/ERP systems and payment acknowledgements.
- Contractor accounting, project management and document management.
- Digital signature, tax, labour, insurance and performance-security workflows.
- Email, spreadsheets, PDF files, calls and physical inspection/measurement records.
- Tender consultants, engineers, accountants and contract/legal advisers.

For each named platform, record product/module, department and jurisdiction, exact capability, access method, contract or public procurement evidence, and the work that remains outside it.

## 7. Aether product slice to test first

**Do not begin with autonomous tender submission or payment.** Start with a read-only/assisted workflow that can prove value safely:

1. Upload a tender pack and its corrigenda.
2. Extract each requirement with exact source/page reference and effective version.
3. Build a qualification/evidence checklist.
4. Match contractor-provided evidence, flag missing/expired/conflicting items.
5. Generate a reviewable bid-pack index and deadline plan.
6. For an awarded contract, import the contract and create milestones/obligations.
7. Preflight a draft bill against contract lines and supplied measurement evidence.
8. Output a discrepancy list with evidence references; do not change official records.
9. Track follow-ups and status only through permitted methods.
10. Measure time, first-pass completeness, rework and missed deadlines against baseline.

This is easier to test than a promise to run the whole contract autonomously. If it proves ROI and earns trust, add authorised connectors and broader execution.

## 8. Pilot measures

- Time to analyse a tender pack and prepare a complete checklist.
- Number of requirements missed or changed after corrigenda.
- Percentage of evidence reusable and still valid.
- First-pass completeness of bid/bill packages.
- Number of bill discrepancies caught before submission.
- Staff/consultant minutes per tender and per bill.
- Number of avoidable queries, returns and repeat follow-ups.
- Time from bill submission to each recorded stage, with blocker categories.
- False-positive discrepancy rate and auditability of every flag.
- Security, confidentiality and procurement-compliance incidents (target zero).

## 9. Unknowns blocking production

- Which works department and contract type will be studied first?
- Which rules, manuals, state circulars and contract conditions govern it?
- What software does that department actually use for measurements, bill passing and payment?
- Which data and status interfaces are authorised?
- Which records can the contractor legally provide, and which require department access?
- Who owns the budget and can sponsor a paid pilot?
- Is the pain mainly tender preparation, bill readiness, status visibility or actual fund/payment delay?
- What is the baseline and measurable improvement that would justify renewal?

## Decision

**Initial product hypothesis:** Aether Contractor Operations — tender-to-bill readiness and contract dependency management, starting with source-linked checklists and evidence reconciliation, then extending into authorised cross-system execution.

**Not yet proven:** willingness to pay, exact price, reduction in bill delay, government-system access, or whether this is the best first commercial wedge. Compare against scholarship exception resolution, developer permit coordination and bank loan-file completeness using measured data before locking the first paid pilot.
